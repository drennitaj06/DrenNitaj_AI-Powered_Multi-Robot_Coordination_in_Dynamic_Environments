## 3. Strengths and weaknesses per component

**A\*.**
- *Strengths:* exact, complete on the snapshot, deterministic; tie-breaking on (f, −g) already favours deep nodes; on open and warehouse maps it expands **exactly** the path (expansions ÷ path length = 1.00, Fig. 1c).
- *Weaknesses:* the Manhattan heuristic ignores walls, so dense random and room maps cost 1.4–7.5× the path length in expansions at 64–128 cells (median); the engine already holds exact static distance fields (`DistanceOracle`) that would be a tighter consistent heuristic.
- **It is not the bottleneck.** In the full A\* system (NFR-PERF-1 verification, workload R) planning takes 0.09 ms of a 0.54 ms mean step; in the slowest 1 % of steps the allocator dominates (3.8 ms of 5.6 ms) (Fig. 14c).
- **It plans for one robot at a time.** `build_constraints` blocks sensed obstacles and their predictions, plus a robot's own stall blocks and the resolver's `avoid` cells; **other robots are not in the constraint set** (`planning/constraints.py`). Robot–robot coordination is left entirely to the controller, the arbiter and the deadlock resolver.

**RRT\*.**
- *Strengths:* a correct anytime planner with documented guarantees; useful as the required sampling-based comparator.
- *Weaknesses:* every call runs **all** `max_iterations` (anytime; `stop_on_first_solution` is off) with a brute-force neighbour index, so a call costs 125–173 ms (n = 2 000) against 0.23 ms for A\*. Its lattice paths are never shorter than A\*'s (E1a). In training, its cost is multiplied by the call rate the controller causes (below).

**Monitor / replanning interface.**
- Any off-path move triggers a full replan (T3). Learned controllers leave the reference path 11–36 times per 100 non-idle robot-steps (block B, A\*); the baseline never does. Learned controllers therefore cause **2–6× more planner calls** than the baseline (A\*, block B: baseline 5.5, matched 6.5, MAPPO 10.8, IPPO 11.3, MADQL 33.1 calls per 100 robot-steps; Fig. 14b). With RRT\* this multiplier, not RRT\* itself, decides feasibility (V-17 pilot: 96–98 % of step time in the planner with the untrained actor).

**Allocation.** Optimal (ε-scaling) or provably ≥ 50 % (sequential); fast enough. Its rare re-auctions are the largest single contributor to the slowest steps (Fig. 14c). Not a quality bottleneck: completion differs by ≤ 0.03 between allocators for every learned policy (full level, shield experiment, descriptive).

**Deadlock resolver.** Worth +0.015 completion with A\* (paired, Holm p = 2.7 × 10⁻⁵); its effect is concentrated on the **static** level (matched baseline: 42 of 67 undelivered tasks there; resolver: 0). Only the baseline uses it.

**Predictive shield.** No detectable completion or wait-ratio cost; removes 60–91 robot–obstacle conflicts per episode for MAPPO 1 and 3 (Fig. 10). It handles robot–obstacle conflicts, not robot–robot ones.

**MADQL.** Weak and unstable at this budget.
- **Greedy symmetric standoffs explain most of the gap.** With ε = 0.05 the same Q-functions complete 0.89–0.92 instead of 0.58–0.66 (Fig. 11); the gain is largest on the static level (0.33 → 0.88), where nothing moves to break a symmetric standoff, and greedy episodes almost never terminate (0–8 of 240 per level, three policies pooled).
- **The rest is learning quality:** stochastic MADQL is still 0.08–0.11 below stochastic PPO; block-A validation jumps by up to 0.25 between checks; the wait ratio is 0.13–0.34.

**MAPPO / IPPO.**
- *Strengths:* fast, stable learning (completion ≥ 0.95 by 100 k steps; consecutive validations within 0.07); the reward weights tested in Option C do not change completion.
- *Weaknesses:* (i) proposes more conflicts than either baseline (IPPO 5–12, MAPPO 17–57 per episode with A\*), mostly robot–robot vertex conflicts for four policies and robot–obstacle vertex conflicts for MAPPO 1 and 3; (ii) greedy execution leaves 0.8–1.8 % of tasks undelivered, again concentrated on the static level (0.977–0.979 vs 1.000 stochastic); (iii) **the completion-first selection rule picks early, conflict-heavy checkpoints** once completion saturates (below).

## 4. Bottlenecks and their evidence

| # | Bottleneck | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Checkpoint selection saturates.** Completion is ≥ 0.98 from ~100 k steps, so the completion-first rule chooses among checkpoints a few tasks apart and ignores conflicts | MAPPO selected at 150–300 k with 9–40 block-A conflicts per episode; a tolerance band of 0.005 on completion would have chosen checkpoints with 1.0–2.3 (stored block-A records, illustrative only, Fig. 12c); MAPPO's block-B conflict excess and the ablation's "effects" both trace to these checkpoints |
| B2 | **Symmetric deadlocks under deterministic execution** | Static-level shortfall for every controller without symmetry breaking: MADQL 0.33, PPO 0.977–0.979, matched baseline 0.967; resolved by the resolver (baseline 1.000) or by sampling (PPO 1.000, MADQL 0.88) |
| B3 | **Robot-blind planning** | Planner constraints exclude other robots; robot–robot conflicts are left to the controller; the reference follower proposes 390 robot–robot vertex conflicts per episode |
| B4 | **Replanning on every deviation** | 2–6× planner calls for learned controllers (T3); makes RRT\* in training infeasible with an untrained actor (36 h projected) |
| B5 | **RRT\* runs its full budget every call** | 125–173 ms per call (E2), whether or not a path was found early; `stop_on_first_solution` is off by default |
| B6 | **MADQL learning instability** | ±0.25 validation swings; 0.08–0.11 below PPO even when sampled |

## 6. Proposed implementation sequence

1. **L1 + L4** (selection rule, trainer hygiene): no training; re-selection from existing checkpoints.
2. **L2** (symmetry breaking): evaluation only; decide the primary execution mode for Issue #8.
3. **A2 → A3** (replanning repair, RRT\* runtime), then re-run the RRT\* pilot and revisit the RRT\*-in-training decision.
4. **A1** (space-time reservations) with **L3** (exact heuristic) — the main planning-level investment.
5. **A4/A5** only if robot–robot conflicts of learned controllers remain clearly above the baseline after steps 1–4.
6. I1–I3 in parallel as small analyses.

**Planning vs PPO tuning.** The records favour improving the planning/coordination interface and the evaluation protocol over further PPO tuning: PPO's learning curves plateau by ~250 k steps; the reward weights in Option C do not change completion; and the two largest remaining deficits — conflict-heavy selected checkpoints (B1) and symmetric standoffs (B2) — are fixed outside the learner. A\*'s *speed* is not a lever (0.09 ms per step); A\*'s *role* is: it currently plans robot-blind (B3) and is re-run on every deviation (B4).

## 8. Validation methodology (applies to every item)

- Pre-register each change (rule, parameters, metrics, comparisons) before running it; new experiment IDs and output directories; never overwrite records.
- Selection on block A only; block B once per decision; test seeds 20 000–20 999 only in Issue #8.
- Paired comparisons on identical instances; paired bootstrap CIs, Wilcoxon, Holm within each metric family; algorithm-level two-level bootstrap without p-values; ≥ 3 training seeds for any claim about a learned method.
- Report completion, absolute conflicts per episode and per delivered task (by type), conflicts per 100 robot-steps (with its denominator), wait ratio, termination, physical collisions, planner calls and step time together — no completion claim without the coordination and cost metrics.
- Regression tests for every algorithmic change (identical traces where behaviour must not change; property tests where it may).

## 9. Risks and possible regressions

- **Protocol drift:** changing the selection rule after seeing block-B results risks tuning on block B. Mitigation: fix δ from block-A reasoning only; evaluate block B once; confirm on test seeds in Issue #8.
- **Behaviour changes for non-learned controllers** (A1, A2) change Issue #3/#4 results; those records stay as they are and new runs get new IDs.
- **Incompleteness of prioritised planning** (A1) can create new deadlocks; keep the resolver and endpoint recovery as fallbacks and test the static level explicitly.
- **Comparability:** obs-v2 (A4) breaks the like-for-like comparison with frozen MADQL; it must be reported as a new configuration, not as "PPO".
- **Stochastic execution** (L2) adds variance; it must be seeded and replicated, and greedy results must stay reported.
- **RRT\* guarantees** (A3): early termination changes the anytime behaviour; the documentation of G5–G9 must be updated if adopted.

## 10. Recommendation for the next phase

Proceed in this order, each as a separately approved step: **(1) a pre-registered checkpoint-selection rule with a completion tolerance band (L1)** and **(2) explicit symmetry breaking at execution (L2)**. Both need no training, address the two largest measured deficits, and make every later comparison more trustworthy. Then **(3) planning-level work: replanning repair, RRT\* runtime and space-time reservations (A2, A3, A1)**, which the evidence suggests will reduce conflicts and standoffs more than further PPO tuning. Retrain learned controllers only after the planning interface changes, under the new selection rule.

Decisions that remain the student's: the `models/` files; RRT\* in training (borderline, V-17); the selection rule (L1); the primary execution mode (L2); and whether MADQL stays a primary comparator or is reported as the Issue #5 baseline learner.
